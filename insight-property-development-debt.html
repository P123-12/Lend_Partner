De-Risking Property Development with Stretch Senior & Mezzanine Debt.

Presales vs equity dilution: structuring capital stacks to maintain developer margin in a fluctuating construction market.

Read Full Analysis ↓
Published: 12.07.26 · 8 min read · Structured Debt Desk

Key Strategic Takeaways

  • The Pre-Sale Impasse: Traditional major bank requirements of 70%–100% pre-sale debt coverage can stall viable construction projects for 12+ months.
  • Stretch Senior Solutions: Non-bank stretch senior facilities provide higher leverage (up to 75% Net Realisable Value) with substantially reduced qualifying pre-sales.
  • Capital Stack Optimization: Utilizing mezzanine debt can preserve developer equity and boost Return on Equity (ROE) far beyond the cost of subordinated capital.

1. The Construction Debt Bottleneck in Australia

The medium-density residential development sector in Australia—comprising townhouses, boutique apartment enclaves, and master-planned sub-divisions—faces unprecedented structural dynamics. While underlying demographic demand for well-located housing remains intense, project commencements are frequently stymied by traditional commercial bank pre-sale thresholds.

Major trading banks typically demand that 70% to 100% of construction debt be pre-covered by unconditional off-the-plan sales with qualifying 10% deposits held in trust. In competitive or cautious buyer markets, achieving these pre-sale quotas can take 12 to 18 months, during which holding costs escalate and builder construction quotes expire.

2. Deconstructing the Capital Stack

Sophisticated property developers look beyond single-lender bank financing by optimizing their capital stack across multiple debt tiers:

  • Traditional Senior Debt: Low cost of capital (major bank margins), but restrictive pre-sale requirements and capped at 60%–65% of Total Development Cost (TDC).
  • Stretch Senior Debt: Offered by institutional non-bank lenders and debt funds, extending to 70%–75% of Gross Realisable Value (GRV) or 85% of TDC, with zero to 40% pre-sale requirements.
  • Mezzanine Finance: Subordinated debt sitting behind senior lenders, allowing developers to reduce their required cash equity contribution down to 10%–15% of project costs.
  • Developer Equity: The developer’s subordinated capital or unencumbered site value.

Developer ROE Analysis: The Mezzanine Advantage

While mezzanine debt carries a higher nominal interest rate (typically 12% to 16% p.a.), the reduced equity requirement drastically accelerates project commencement and magnifies the developer’s Return on Equity (ROE). Starting construction 8 months earlier also mitigates the risk of escalating tender prices from builders.

3. Mitigating Construction Delivery and Builder Risk

Lenders evaluate builder viability with greater rigour than ever before. To secure competitive non-bank construction facilities, developers must prepare comprehensive institutional dossiers including:

  1. Fixed-Price D&C Contracts: Guaranteed maximum price design and construct contracts with realistic contingency reserves (typically 5% to 7.5% of hard costs).
  2. Independent Quantity Surveyor (QS) Reports: Thorough initial reviews and scheduled monthly progressive draw inspections.
  3. Builder Balance Sheet Due Diligence: Reviewing the general contractor’s current work in progress (WIP) and liquidity to eliminate counterparty failure risk.

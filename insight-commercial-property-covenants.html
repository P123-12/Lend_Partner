Commercial Property in 2026: Navigating Covenants & WALE Metrics.

How interest rate normalization and tighter bank underwriting make early debt structuring essential for commercial landlords.

Read Full Analysis ↓
Published: 04.08.26 · 7 min read · Commercial Lending Desk

Key Strategic Takeaways

  • ICR Dominance: Interest Cover Ratios (ICR) are now the primary bottleneck for commercial refinancing, superseding Loan-to-Value (LVR) headroom.
  • WALE Sensitivity: Lenders are applying strict haircut parameters to lease profiles with Weighted Average Lease Expiry (WALE) under 3 years.
  • Alternative Capital Tiers: Non-bank and private credit funds are stepping into commercial property refinances with covenant-light terms.

1. The Shifting Landscape of Australian Commercial Lending

Commercial real estate investors in Australia have navigated a substantial recalibration in credit metrics over the past 24 months. While historical commercial lending focused primarily on asset valuation and Loan-to-Value Ratios (LVR) capped at 65% to 70%, major institutional lenders have pivoted decisively toward cashflow covenants, liquidity reserves, and tenant durability.

Today, a commercial office building, industrial warehouse, or healthcare precinct can boast an immaculate balance sheet and an LVR of 55%, yet still encounter acute resistance from traditional bank credit committees if its Interest Cover Ratio (ICR) or Weighted Average Lease Expiry (WALE) falls outside tightening parameters.

2. Understanding ICR and DSCR Stress Testing

The Interest Cover Ratio measures the operational net rental income of a property against the annual interest servicing expense. Prior to interest rate rises, an ICR covenant of 1.40x to 1.50x was readily achievable when debt costs sat around 3.5%.

At commercial debt rates between 6.5% and 7.5%, the required net rental yield to maintain an ICR above 1.50x has expanded significantly. Lenders are now imposing:

  • Higher buffer interest rates (+1.50% to +2.00%) applied to existing commercial debt;
  • Demands for amortization (principal reduction of 1% to 2% per annum) rather than interest-only terms;
  • Debt Service Cover Ratios (DSCR) that scrutinise capital expenditure and tenant incentive obligations.

WALE Metrics: The Valuation and Credit Threshold

The Weighted Average Lease Expiry (WALE) measures the average remaining duration of tenant leases weighted by gross or net lettable income. Tier-1 banks typically seek a WALE of at least 3.0 to 5.0 years for commercial investment debt. When WALE slips below 2.5 years, traditional banks may impose cash sweeps, where surplus tenant rental income is impounded in an offset reserve rather than distributed to owners.

3. Strategic Solutions for Commercial Landlords

Commercial property owners facing covenant scrutiny or upcoming loan expiries must take proactive steps 6 to 12 months ahead of maturity:

  1. Pre-emptive Lease Extensions: Commencing tenant renewal discussions early to lengthen the WALE profile ahead of formal lender valuation.
  2. Non-Bank & Private Credit Transitions: Australian private debt funds and non-bank commercial lenders frequently offer facilities with lower ICR hurdles (1.10x–1.25x) or interest capitalisation options, providing runway while asset leasing programs are finalised.
  3. Mezzanine Capital or Second Mortgages: Introducing subordinated debt to bridge equity shortfalls without requiring forced property disposals during market transitions.

4. Structuring Commercial Facilities for the Next Cycle

At Lend Partners, we advise commercial landlords on structuring multi-tranche facilities across Australia’s top 40 institutional and private capital providers. By balancing covenant flexibility against headline interest margins, commercial borrowers maintain asset control and liquidity through fluctuating economic cycles.
